## 1. Governor's Cup Competition Overview

The Governor's Cup is Kentucky's premier interscholastic academic competition, organized annually by the KAAC across three age divisions:
1. **Elementary Division**: Grades 4–5
2. **Sixth Grade Division**: Grade 6 (includes the Sixth Grade Showcase / JV Challenge)
3. **Middle Grades Division**: Grades 6–8

### 1.1 Competition Event Formats
- **Quick Recall**: Fast-paced oral buzzer competition between two 4-player teams. Rounds contain an equal distribution of questions across the five academic subject areas:
  1. Mathematics (20%)
  2. Science (20%)
  3. Social Studies (20%)
  4. Language Arts (20%)
  5. Arts and Humanities (20%)
- **Content Assessment (Written Exams)**: Rigorous multiple-choice exams (typically 50 questions plus tiebreakers) taken individually in each of the five subject areas.

## 2. Subject Syllabus & Curriculum Matrix

### 2.1 Elementary Curriculum (Grades 4–5)

#### Mathematics
1. **Properties of Real Numbers**: Comparing and ordering real numbers.
2. **Data Collection & Interpretation**: Graphs, frequency tables, charts (Written Assessment only).
3. **Factors & Multiples**: GCF, LCM, real-world word problems using whole numbers and decimals.
4. **Fractions & Mixed Numbers**: Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division in real-world contexts.
5. **Ratios, Proportions & Percents**: Problem-solving with rates, unit prices, simple proportions.
6. **Measurement Systems**: Conversions within Standard (US customary) and Metric systems.
7. **Coordinate Plane**: Graphing ordered pairs in Quadrant I (Written Assessment only).
8. **Geometric Figures**: Classifying 2D lines, angles (acute, right, obtuse), polygons, and quadrilaterals.
9. **Perimeter, Area & Volume**: Perimeter of polygons, area of rectangles/triangles, volume of rectangular prisms.
10. **Symmetry, Congruence & Similarity** (Written Assessment only).
11. **Probability**: Counting principles, spinners, dice (number cubes), coins, random drawings.
12. **Algebraic Thinking**: Numerical expressions, order of operations (PEMDAS), number patterns.
13. **Pre-Algebra**: Simple one-step equations and unknowns.

#### Science
1. **Properties of Objects & Materials**: States of matter, density, physical properties.
2. **Position & Motion**: Speed, direction, forces, pushes/pulls, gravity, friction.
3. **Energy & Magnetism**: Light, heat, sound, static/current electricity, magnetic poles and fields.
4. **Earth Science (Geology)**: Rocks (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic), minerals, soil layers, fossils.
5. **Earth Science (Subsystems)**: Hydrosphere, atmosphere, water cycle, weather instruments, climate, seasons.
6. **Solar System**: Sun, moon phases, planets in order, planetary characteristics, rotation vs. revolution.
7. **Organisms (Cells & Simple Life)**: Basic cell parts, bacteria, life cycles, organism relationships.
8. **Organisms (Plants)**: Photosynthesis, parts of plants/flowers, seeds, plant life cycles.
9. **Organisms (Invertebrates)**: Insects, arachnids, crustaceans, mollusks, worms, life cycles.
10. **Organisms (Vertebrates)**: Fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals, adaptations.
11. **Human Body Systems**: Skeletal, muscular, circulatory, respiratory, digestive, nervous systems, health.
12. **Scientific Inquiry**: Scientific method, hypotheses, variables, laboratory tools (graduated cylinder, balance, microscope).
13. **Famous Scientists & Innovators Rotation**:
    - *Core Focus*: Albert Einstein, Isaac Newton, Galileo Galilei, Charles Darwin, Gregor Mendel, Marie Curie, Rachel Carson, Thomas Edison, Benjamin Franklin, Edward Jenner.
    - *2027 Rotation*: Jane Goodall, Louis Pasteur, Florence Nightingale, Yuri Gagarin, George Washington Carver.
    - *2028 Rotation*: Valentina Tereshkova, Jonas Salk, Charles Drew, Tim Berners-Lee, Elizabeth Blackwell.
    - *2029 Rotation*: Neil Armstrong, Johannes Kepler, Katherine Johnson, Ellen Ochoa, Nicolaus Copernicus.

#### Social Studies
1. **Kentucky Geography**: Regions of Kentucky (Bluegrass, Knobs, Eastern Coal Field, Western Coal Field, Jackson Purchase, Pennyroyal), rivers (Ohio, Kentucky, Mississippi), borders.
2. **Kentucky History**: Daniel Boone, Wilderness Road, statehood (1792, 15th state), Frankfort capital, major industries (coal, agriculture, equine).
3. **US Geography (Physical)**: Mountain ranges (Appalachians, Rockies), major rivers, Great Lakes, Plains.
4. **US Geography (Human)**: Population trends, urbanization, land use, natural resource interaction.
5. **Early US Cultures**: Native American nations, early European exploration (Spanish, French, English), West African heritage.
6. **US History (Colonial to Revolution)**: 13 colonies, Boston Tea Party, Declaration of Independence, leaders (George Washington, Thomas Jefferson).
7. **US History (Revolutionary War to 1865)**: Constitution, War of 1812, Westward expansion, Civil War, Abraham Lincoln.
8. **US History (Reconstruction to Present)**: Industrial Revolution, World Wars, Civil Rights movement, modern era.
9. **Government**: 3 branches (Executive, Legislative, Judicial), checks and balances, local/state/federal structure.
10. **Citizenship**: Bill of Rights, duties vs. responsibilities, voting.
11. **Basic Economics**: Goods, services, supply, demand, scarcity, producers, consumers, trade.
12. **US Symbols & Documents**: Liberty Bell, Star-Spangled Banner, Statue of Liberty, Constitution, Emancipation Proclamation.

#### Language Arts
1. **Newbery Medal Winners**: Medal winners from the past 20 years.
2. **Caldecott Medal Winners**: Caldecott winners from the past 20 years.
3. **Classic Children's Literature**: Authors, works, characters (e.g., *Charlotte's Web*, *The Secret Garden*, *Peter Pan*).
4. **Famous Poetry & Poetic Forms**: Stanza, rhyme scheme, haiku, limerick, free verse, Shel Silverstein, Jack Prelutsky.
5. **Famous Authors Rotation**:
    - *Core Focus*: Gary Paulsen, Roald Dahl, Lewis Carroll, Louisa May Alcott, Lois Lowry, Laura Ingalls Wilder, Beverly Cleary, Norton Juster, Katherine Paterson, C.S. Lewis.
    - *2027 Rotation*: Robert C. O'Brien, Rick Riordan, Louis Sachar.
    - *2028 Rotation*: Jerry Spinelli, Russell Freedman, Rita Williams-Garcia.
    - *2029 Rotation*: Christopher Paul Curtis, Pam Muñoz Ryan, Joseph Bruchac.
6. **Literature Genres & Devices**: Simile, metaphor, personification, hyperbole, alliteration, onomatopoeia, theme.
7. **Folklore, Tall Tales & Legends**: Paul Bunyan, Pecos Bill, John Henry, Johnny Appleseed, American legends.
8. **Nursery Rhymes & Famous Speeches**: Traditional rhymes, Gettysburg Address, "I Have a Dream".
9. **Vocabulary & Etymology**: Greek and Latin roots, prefixes, suffixes, synonyms, antonyms.
10. **Grammar & Mechanics**: Punctuation, capitalization, parts of speech (subjects, direct/indirect objects, predicates).
11. **Sentence Structure**: Simple, compound, complex sentences; clauses and phrases.
12. **Spelling & Word Study**: Homophones, homonyms, compound words.

#### Arts and Humanities
1. **Elements of Music**: Rhythm, tempo, pitch, timbre, melody, harmony, dynamics (piano, forte, crescendo).
2. **Purposes & Styles of Music**: Ceremonial, recreational, artistic expression; folk, jazz, classical, bluegrass.
3. **Musical Instruments**: Families (strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion) and their sound generation.
4. **Famous Composers**: George Gershwin, Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, Aaron Copland, Ludwig van Beethoven, Franz Schubert, Johann Sebastian Bach.
5. **Elements & Styles of Dance**: Space, time, force; ballet, tap, jazz, modern, square dance, folk dance.
6. **Dancers & Choreographers**: Famous figures in ballet and American dance.
7. **Drama & Theatre**: Elements of drama, stage directions (stage left, upstage), script conventions, age-appropriate Broadway musicals (*The Lion King*, *Annie*, *The Sound of Music*).
8. **Visual Arts Elements**: Line, shape, form, color (primary, secondary, complementary), texture, space, value.
9. **Principles of Design**: Balance, contrast, emphasis, movement, pattern, rhythm, unity.
10. **Famous Artists & Masterpieces**: Michelangelo, Georges Seurat, Mary Cassatt, Pablo Picasso, Claude Monet, Leonardo da Vinci.
11. **World Cultures & Traditions**: West African, Native American, Colonial American art, dance, and music.

### 2.2 Sixth Grade Curriculum

#### Mathematics
1. **Fractions, Decimals, Percents**: Operations, improper fractions, percents greater than 100%.
2. **Ratios & Proportions**: Unit rate, proportional comparisons, equivalent ratios.
3. **Number Theory**: Prime vs. composite, prime factorization, GCF, LCM.
4. **Rational Numbers**: Integers, absolute value, negative coordinates on full 4-quadrant plane.
5. **Properties of Operations**: Commutative, associative, distributive, identity, inverse properties.
6. **Algebraic Expressions**: Evaluating expressions, writing expressions from word prompts, variables.
7. **Equations & Inequalities**: One-variable linear equations and inequalities, graphing on number lines.
8. **2D Geometry**: Area of triangles, special quadrilaterals (trapezoids, parallelograms), irregular polygons.
9. **3D Geometry**: Surface area and volume of rectangular prisms, triangular prisms, pyramids.
10. **Statistics & Data**: Mean, median, mode, range, interquartile range (IQR), box plots, histograms.
11. **Probability**: Theoretical vs. experimental probability, compound events, tree diagrams.

#### Science
1. **Matter & Chemistry**: Atoms, elements, compounds, mixtures, physical/chemical changes, periodic table basics.
2. **Forces & Motion**: Newton’s Three Laws of Motion, balanced/unbalanced forces, velocity, acceleration, gravity, friction.
3. **Energy**: Kinetic, potential, thermal, radiant, electrical, law of conservation of energy.
4. **Cellular Biology**: Cell theory, organelle functions (nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplasts, cell wall), single vs. multicellular.
5. **Heredity & Genetics**: DNA, genes, dominant vs. recessive traits, Punnett squares.
6. **Ecosystems**: Energy pyramids, food webs, trophic levels, symbiosis, carrying capacity.
7. **Earth Subsystems**: Plate tectonics, rock cycle, crust/mantle/core, earthquakes, volcanoes, atmosphere layers.
8. **Space Science**: Moon phases, eclipses (solar/lunar), tides, planets, comets, asteroids, constellations.
9. **Scientific Process & Safety**: Controlled experiments, independent/dependent variables, lab safety.

#### Social Studies
1. **Current Events**: Key global and national events from the most recent 5 years.
2. **Geography Skills**: Latitude/longitude, topographic maps, projections, global hemispheres.
3. **Ancient River Valley Civilizations**: Mesopotamia (Tigris-Euphrates), Egypt (Nile), Indus Valley, Ancient China (Yellow River).
4. **Classical Civilizations**: Ancient Greece (Athens, Sparta, democracy, philosophy), Ancient Rome (Republic, Empire, Pax Romana).
5. **Age of Exploration**: European explorers (Columbus, Magellan, da Gama, Hudson, Cartier).
6. **Early America**: Native American regional cultures, 13 Colonies, French and Indian War.
7. **American Revolution & Early Republic**: Declaration of Independence, Articles of Confederation, US Constitution, War of 1812.
8. **Presidents & Leaders**: Washington, Adams, Jefferson, Madison, Lincoln.
9. **Economics**: Market economies, trade barriers, currency, supply/demand curves.

#### Language Arts
1. **Newbery Award Winners**: Historical and contemporary recipients.
2. **Classic Novels**: Mark Twain, Jack London, Jules Verne, Frances Hodgson Burnett.
3. **Poetry & Plays**: Shakespeare (*A Midsummer Night's Dream*, *Romeo and Juliet* basics), Arthur Miller, Thornton Wilder (*Our Town*).
4. **Short Stories**: Edgar Allan Poe, O. Henry, Guy de Maupassant, Ray Bradbury.
5. **Grammar & Mechanics**: Sentence structures (run-ons, fragments), clauses (independent/dependent), verb tenses, pronoun-antecedent agreement.
6. **Literary Devices**: Irony (verbal, situational, dramatic), foreshadowing, symbolism, theme, tone vs. mood.

#### Arts and Humanities
1. **Visual Artists**: Michelangelo, Albrecht Dürer, Vincent van Gogh, Pablo Picasso, Rembrandt, Claude Monet, Jackson Pollock, Mary Cassatt, Andy Warhol, Grant Wood, Piet Mondrian, Edward Hopper, Norman Rockwell.
2. **Architecture**: Classical orders (Doric, Ionic, Corinthian), arches, domes, Frank Lloyd Wright, Frank Gehry.
3. **Composers**: Mozart, Handel, Wagner, Debussy, Bach, Beethoven, Tchaikovsky, Copland, Gershwin, John Williams, Leonard Bernstein, Scott Joplin.
4. **World Religions**: Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism (origins, holy texts, holidays, key beliefs).
5. **Mythology**: Greek and Roman pantheons, major myths, monsters (Minotaur, Medusa, Cerberus).

### 2.3 Middle Grades Curriculum (Grades 6–8)

#### Mathematics
1. **Number Theory & Sequences**: Arithmetic/geometric sequences, repeating decimals to fractions.
2. **Ratios, Rates & Percents**: Multi-step percent problems (tax, tip, discount, markups, percent change).
3. **Coordinate Geometry**: Slopes of lines ($m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$), slope-intercept form ($y = mx + b$).
4. **Systems of Linear Equations**: Substitution, elimination, graphing intersections.
5. **Advanced 2D & 3D Geometry**: Pythagorean theorem ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$), special right triangles ($45^\circ-45^\circ-90^\circ$, $30^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ$), basic trigonometry ($\sin, \cos, \tan$).
6. **Surface Area & Volume**: Spheres ($V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$), cones ($V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$), cylinders ($V = \pi r^2 h$), pyramids.
7. **Polynomials & Factoring**: Multiplying binomials (FOIL), factoring quadratics ($x^2 + bx + c$).
8. **Quadratic Equations**: Factoring, completing the square, quadratic formula.
9. **Exponents & Radicals**: Laws of exponents, negative/fractional exponents, simplifying radicals.
10. **Permutations & Combinations**: $_n P_r$, $_n C_r$, multi-stage probability.

#### Science
1. **Chemistry**: Atomic structure (protons, neutrons, electrons), valence electrons, chemical bonding (covalent vs. ionic), balancing chemical equations.
2. **Physics & Waves**: Wave properties (wavelength, frequency, amplitude, wave speed), electromagnetic spectrum, Doppler effect, refraction/reflection.
3. **Cell Biology & Genetics**: Mitosis vs. meiosis, transcription/translation, genetic mutations, pedigree charts.
4. **Evolution & Ecology**: Natural selection, fossil record evidence, homologous vs. analogous structures, ecological succession (primary vs. secondary).
5. **Earth & Planetary Systems**: Plate boundaries (convergent, divergent, transform), rock age dating (strata, radiometric), climate change factors, stellar lifecycle (protostar to white dwarf/supernova).
6. **Microbiology**: Bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, immune system response (antibodies, vaccines).

#### Social Studies
1. **World Civilizations**:
   - Medieval Europe: Feudalism, Magna Carta (1215), Black Death, Crusades.
   - Asian Empires: Tang, Song, Ming Dynasties; Mongol Empire; Feudal Japan (samurai, shogun).
   - Islamic Empires: Golden Age of Islam, caliphates, trade routes.
   - African Kingdoms: Ghana, Mali (Mansa Musa), Songhai.
   - Pre-Columbian Americas: Maya, Aztec, Inca civilizations.
2. **US History (1815–1877)**: Monroe Doctrine, Jacksonian Democracy, Mexican-American War, Abolitionist movement, Civil War battles (Gettysburg, Antietam), Reconstruction.
3. **Government & Civics**: Judicial review (*Marbury v. Madison*), Constitutional amendments (especially 1st–10th, 13th, 14th, 15th, 19th), Supreme Court milestone cases.
4. **Economics**: Macroeconomics, fiscal vs. monetary policy, GDP, inflation, Federal Reserve system.

#### Language Arts
1. **Annual Focus Work (Crucial for Content Assessment)**:
   - **2027 Focus Work**: ***Animal Farm* by George Orwell** (Allegory, characters [Old Major, Napoleon, Snowball, Boxer, Squealer], historical parallels to Russian Revolution, themes of power, propaganda, corruption).
2. **Award-Winning Literature**:
   - National Book Award in Young People's Literature
   - Scott O'Dell Award for Historical Fiction
   - Coretta Scott King Award
   - Sibert and YALSA Nonfiction awards
3. **World Literature**: Homer (*The Odyssey*, *The Iliad*), Dante, Cervantes (*Don Quixote*), Shakespeare tragedies and comedies.
4. **Grammar & Verbals**: Participles, gerunds, infinitives; active vs. passive voice; subjunctive mood; misplaced/dangling modifiers.
5. **Foreign Words & Phrases in English**: *Carpe diem*, *status quo*, *quid pro quo*, *faux pas*, *deja vu*, *bona fide*, *ad hoc*.

#### Arts and Humanities
1. **Visual Arts Masters**: Michelangelo, Dürer, Velázquez, van Gogh, Picasso, Rembrandt, Monet, Goya, Cassatt, Warhol, Wood, Mondrian, Hopper, Rockwell.
2. **Architects**: Andrea Palladio, Frank Lloyd Wright, Filippo Brunelleschi, Frank Gehry, Walter Gropius, Michelangelo, Christopher Wren.
3. **Music Periods & Masters**:
   - Baroque (Bach, Vivaldi, Handel)
   - Classical (Mozart, Haydn, early Beethoven)
   - Romantic (late Beethoven, Chopin, Tchaikovsky, Wagner, Brahms)
   - 20th Century / American (Copland, Gershwin, Bernstein, Joplin, John Williams)
4. **Dance**: Martha Graham, George Balanchine, Bob Fosse, Jerome Robbins, Misty Copeland, Isadora Duncan, Vaslav Nijinsky, Savion Glover.
5. **Mythology**: Complete Greek, Roman, and Norse pantheons.
6. **Cultural Representatives Rotation**:
   - **2027 Rotation**: Old Faithful, Great Wall of China, Mickey Mouse, Spider-Man, Legend of Zelda, The Beatles, Dolly Parton, Muhammad Ali, Lionel Messi, ChatGPT, Princess Diana, Eiffel Tower, Star Wars, The Simpsons, A Christmas Story.
   - **2028 Rotation**: Mardi Gras, Mt. Fuji, Wonder Woman, Godzilla, Transformers, Taylor Swift, Bill Monroe, Michael Jordan, Babe Ruth, Barbie, Atlantis, Sasquatch/Yeti, James Bond, Betty Boop, The Addams Family.

## 3. Question Schema & Storing Architecture

Each question in QUEST: Kentucky conforms to the following strict, type-safe schema:

```json
{
  "id": "q_math_el_001",
  "division": "elementary",
  "subject": "mathematics",
  "topic": "fractions_and_decimals",
  "curriculumCode": "EL-MATH-04",
  "gradeBand": "4-5",
  "prompt": "What is the value of 3/4 + 2/3 expressed as a mixed number in simplest terms?",
  "choices": [
    {"id": "a", "text": "1 5/12"},
    {"id": "b", "text": "5/7"},
    {"id": "c", "text": "1 1/12"},
    {"id": "d", "text": "1 7/12"}
  ],
  "answerKey": "a",
  "explanation": "To add 3/4 and 2/3, find the common denominator 12: 3/4 = 9/12 and 2/3 = 8/12. 9/12 + 8/12 = 17/12 = 1 5/12.",
  "difficulty": 2,
  "provenance": {
    "type": "original_aligned",
    "curriculumReference": "KAAC Elementary Math #4",
    "rightsStatus": "approved"
  },
  "reviewStatus": "verified"
}
```

> **Security Mandate**: `answerKey` and `explanation` remain on the server in Cloudflare Workers / D1 and are NEVER transmitted to the client before the player submits their attempt.
